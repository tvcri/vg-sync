# Context brief, 2026-11-11

What was known when this repo was created. It is distilled from the work in
`vg-utils` (`lgl-cleanup`, `lgl-import`, `mailchimp`) and `mailchimp-poc`
between 2026-07 and 2026-11. It also records the decisions made on
2026-11-11, the day the repo was created. It is a starting point for the
design, not a design. Counts are from the dates given and will have moved.

## What is wanted

A service that runs on a schedule with no staff interaction and keeps LGL,
Village Green (VG) and Mailchimp in step. A new donor in LGL becomes a person
in VG. People in VG reach the Mailchimp audience. The rules for duplicates,
conflicts and bad data are ours to define; there is no existing manual process
worth copying, because what staff did by hand was ad hoc.

Board card: VG-202611-521 (issue 153 in `tvcri/village-green`).

**The customer asked for LGL and VG to be "two-way", and agreed Mailchimp is one-way.** That is the customer's
word and its detail is not defined. Do not call it a mirror: a mirror is a
one-way copy, which is not what was asked.

So there are three legs:

| Leg | Direction | State today |
|---|---|---|
| LGL → VG | donors become or link to VG people; changes made in LGL reach VG | first contact done once by hand (`lgl-import`), sheet-driven |
| VG → LGL | changes made in VG reach LGL | never done; nothing writes contact data to LGL |
| VG → Mailchimp | one-way; nothing flows back | done by hand twice (`mailchimp-poc`), plus LGL → Mailchimp directly (`vg-utils/mailchimp`) |

## Foundational rule: who belongs in LGL

**LGL holds only donors, and members who pay their dues through LGL's PayPal
integration.** This is the customer's rule, stated 2026-09-29, and it is why
LGL was pruned on 2026-10-02. Everything else follows from it:

- **The service never creates a constituent in LGL.** A VG member or volunteer
  who has never given and does not pay dues by PayPal does not belong there.
  People enter LGL by giving or by paying dues, which happens in LGL itself.
- VG → LGL therefore only updates constituents that already exist and are
  linked to a VG person.
- LGL is a subset of the people VG knows about; VG is not a subset of LGL.

In code terms (`vg-utils/lgl-cleanup/src/classify.js`): a constituent belongs
if they have any counting gift (not Pledge, Soft Credit or Peer Credit). Dues
are a gift to the Membership fund paid by PayPal. "Donor" means at least one
counting gift that is not dues.

## Decided

Decisions marked (engineer) are engineering calls. The rest are the
customer's, or follow from the customer's rules.

Placement and shape:

- This is its own repo, built and deployed like `vg-email-sidecar`: an image
  from CI, run on the prod VM beside the app.
- **One repo, two separate executables:** LGL ↔ VG, and VG → Mailchimp. They
  share no code and simply run one after the other. (engineer)
- **LGL ↔ VG is designed and built first.** The Mailchimp executable is a
  later phase with its own questions. (engineer)
- Not in the app: third-party keys and outbound calls stay out of the API
  process, and sync fixes should not wait on app releases.

How it reaches VG (engineer):

- **Through the VG API, never the database.** The audit trail is required:
  every change the service makes must appear in `audit_event` under its own
  identity.
- **It signs in as its own Keycloak service-account client**, which the
  engineer will create. The import's device-flow sign-in is for a person and
  does not apply.
- Reads go through the API as well, so there is one access path.
- The link already exists in the app: the `donor` table (person id, LGL
  constituent id, last gift date; migration 0032) and
  `PUT`/`DELETE /persons/{personId}/donor`, scope `vg:donor`, permission
  `donor:write`.

What the sync remembers (engineer):

- **Sync memory lives in the VG database**, in a new table added by an app
  migration and reached through new API endpoints. It is keyed on person id
  so it can be joined for analysis, and it travels with prod backups and
  restored snapshots, so memory and people always describe the same moment.
- It holds, per linked pair, the field values as they stood at the last sync.
- A sync log, also in VG: one row per decision the service made, including
  "did nothing". VG's audit trail covers writes to VG; this is the only
  record of writes to LGL.

Who is in scope:

- **Donors only.** A linked pair is a VG person with a `donor` row and the LGL
  constituent it points to. Donor means a counting gift that is not PayPal
  Membership dues, the same test the cleanup, the Mailchimp tool and the
  import use, edge cases accepted.
- **Dues-only members are left alone.** They are in LGL through the PayPal
  integration and in VG as members, with nothing linking the two. If one later
  gives, they are matched to their existing VG person and linked.
- **Organizations are skipped until VG has a model for them.** (engineer)
  That model has its own board item. Do not raise organizations with the
  customer as part of this work.
- The service never creates a constituent in LGL (see the foundational rule).

Who edits what:

- **Staff edit both systems.** Once the sync is live, staff will record a
  gift and a correction in LGL and expect VG to catch up, and will correct
  people in VG and expect LGL to catch up. The earlier instruction to leave
  LGL alone was a temporary block until the sync exists; duplicates left in
  LGL meanwhile are the sync's problem.
- **Business rules are the customer's, not the engineer's.** The design must
  not hard-code them. See the engine below.

Customer requirements (stated by customer staff, 2026-11-04 and 11-05):

- Donors must be visible in VG, with mailing labels and email campaigns
  generated for them. "LGL-only people stay in LGL" is a non-starter.
- VG marks a person as a Donor. **Gift history stays out of VG entirely.**
- VG holds a **last gift date**. The customer wants to reach back five years
  to re-engage lapsed donors.
- Donors, and the fact that someone is a donor, are visible to **Hub Staff
  only**, not village-level users. `hasStaffAccess` in the app passes any role
  grant and is too broad for this.
- Anyone Dropped in VG (member, volunteer or both) gets no email and no mail.
  This is a mailing rule. A dropped member and a lapsed donor are different
  groups; someone can be both, and that case is undecided.

## The engine, as far as it has been discussed

Not yet a design. This is the shape agreed in conversation.

Each run:

1. Pull LGL in full (about 95 seconds), read the linked people from VG, read
   the sync memory.
2. For each linked pair and each field, compare three values: LGL now, VG
   now, and the value at the last sync. The result is one of: no change, LGL
   changed, VG changed, both changed.
3. Look the field and case up in a **policy table** and do what it says: copy
   one way, copy the other, or do nothing and log.
4. After a successful write, update the memory for that field. A field left
   in disagreement keeps its old memory and is re-examined every run.
5. For LGL donors with no link, apply the import's matching rules: link,
   create, or hold and log.

Principles:

- **Business rules are rows in the policy table, not code.** A customer
  answer, or a change of mind, is an edit to the table.
- **The safe default is "do nothing and log it"**, for every case the
  customer has not ruled on.
- **Dry run by default.** Nothing is written unless the run is told to.
- **Poll, do not wait for a push.** LGL has no outbound webhooks as far as
  its documentation shows (see LGL API below).

Known hard spots:

- **Couples.** One LGL constituent can be linked to two VG people. A change
  to either person has to be reconciled against one LGL record.
- **Addresses.** LGL holds a list per constituent and VG holds one address,
  so a write to LGL must choose which entry it is updating (see LGL data
  below).

## With the customer

Four business questions were put on a one-page sheet for the customer on
2026-11-11, each with a suggested answer. The sheet is not in the repo. Until
answered, each is "do nothing and log":

1. **The same field changed in both systems** between runs. Suggested: the
   more recent change wins and the losing value is logged.
2. **LGL mails a donor somewhere other than the address in VG.** Suggested:
   leave the address alone on both sides for those people and list them.
   Measured 2026-11-11: 28 of 765 donors (24 two street addresses, 4 a PO box
   in LGL; in 11 of the 28, LGL also holds VG's address as a second entry).
   That was judged too few to justify giving VG a second address, which would
   touch every consumer of a person, mailing labels included.
3. **Deceased in one system.** Suggested: mark the other, either direction,
   and never unmark automatically.
4. **A donor deleted or merged in LGL.** Suggested: never delete a person in
   either system; follow the surviving record after a merge; if a donor is
   deleted from LGL, keep the VG person and drop the Donor mark.

Also suggested to the customer: one run a night.

## Open engineering questions

1. **The policy table.** Its shape, where it lives, and which fields it
   covers (name, address, email, phone, deceased, last gift date).
2. **The sync memory and log tables**, and the API endpoints for them, in
   `village-green`.
3. **Mapping VG's one address onto LGL's list.** Which entry corresponds, and
   what a VG change does to it.
4. **Couples.** How one LGL record and two VG people are compared and written.
5. **Unlinked donors.** How much of the import's sheet-driven matching can
   run unattended, and what "hold" means with no staff to resolve it. Staff
   decisions from the import review must not be undone.
6. **Detecting which change is more recent**, if the customer chooses that
   rule. LGL has `updated_at` on constituents and on each address; VG has
   `audit_event`.
7. **New people created in VG** get no town or legislative districts unless
   the app resolves them on create.
8. **Deploy.** `vm-sidecar-deploy` handles exactly one service. Generalize it
   or add a sibling. Scheduled job versus long-lived process with a timer.
9. **Failure reporting.** Logs and an Azure alert are the existing pattern.

Parked for the Mailchimp phase: whether that executable only adds, or also
updates, tags and removes. Everything so far has been additive on purpose.

## What was learned the hard way

Matching people between LGL and VG:

- **Never trust an email match without the name.** Shared emails are common
  and are often a third-party payer (a relative paying dues, a power of
  attorney), not the same person. `lgl-cleanup/src/match.js` trusted a single
  email match and linked clearly different people; one result was a false
  deceased flag.
- "Shared email, different name" is usually a nickname, not a new person.
  VG's first name often holds "First (Nick)", and VG has a nickname field.
  Match on both.
- A name-only match that agrees on house number and ZIP is a strong confirmer.
- Couples: LGL can hold a couple in one record. The import split on "and"/"&"
  and gave both partners the same email, phone and address.
  `lglConstituentId` in VG is deliberately non-unique so two VG people can
  share one LGL id.
- Shared household emails in VG are normal, not a data problem.
- A staff "already correct" result in a review does not mean "same person" or
  "untouched". Do not infer identity from it.
- Staff decisions from the import review (who is the same person, who to
  skip) exist in a sheet. The sync should not undo them.

LGL data:

- There is no single "a gift was made" property. `gift_type_name` is the best
  indicator. Amount and date fields are filled on pledges too.
- **Donor rule in use:** at least one counting gift (not Pledge, Soft Credit
  or Peer Credit) that is not PayPal dues. Dues = a gift to the Membership
  fund paid by PayPal. Dues-only payers are not donors. "Donor" and "Dues" are
  our derived labels, not LGL properties. Source: `lgl-cleanup/src/classify.js`.
- Installment rows with no received date look like scheduled payments, not
  money received; `classify.js` counts them. Unresolved.
- Ticket purchases cannot be separated from donations before 2024-09.
- "Anonymous" placeholder records are not people and are never imported. The
  `is_anon` flag on a real donor is a different thing; those import.
- LGL membership levels are leftovers from an earlier system. Ignore them.
- Useful fields: `external_constituent_id` (population unchecked),
  `is_deceased`, `deceased_date`, `updated_at`, and per-address
  `is_preferred`, `not_current`, `seasonal`.
- LGL values need cleaning before they enter VG: state names instead of
  codes, names and cities typed in one case, malformed addresses, emails and
  phones. `lgl-import/src/person.js` does this and leaves out what it cannot
  fix.

LGL addresses (snapshot of 2026-11-09, 1,709 addresses on 1,310 constituents):

- A constituent has a list of addresses. Each has a type (only Home, Work or
  Other in this account), `is_preferred` (the one LGL mails to), `not_current`,
  and `verified` with `verified_on`. There is no separate mailing and physical
  pair. 219 constituents have more than one current address.
- LGL's built-in address verification (Smarty) is switched on. 1,203
  addresses are verified and carry a ZIP+4; 1,032 of those are current and
  preferred. The sync could carry the verified form into VG for linked donors.
- **Verified does not mean preferred.** Verifying never changes the preferred
  flag. 152 verified current addresses are not preferred, most of them added
  in three bulk loads; 204 preferred addresses are unverified; 55
  constituents have an unverified preferred address beside a verified one,
  and for 36 of them it is the same address twice. So the preferred address
  is not reliably the best one LGL has.

LGL API:

- **No outbound webhooks were found.** LGL's webhook feature is an inbound
  listener, and its Zapier help page says data cannot be pushed out of an
  account. Only the first quarter of the API reference page was read, so a
  question to LGL support would settle it. Third-party "new constituent"
  triggers are polling.
- Constituent search accepts `updated_from` and `updated_to`, so a run can
  ask for only what changed since the last one.

- A full pull of constituents and gifts is about 7 bulk requests and 95
  seconds (about 1,300 constituents, 4,300 gifts on 2026-11-04). Cheap enough
  that incremental sync on `updated_at` may not be needed for reads.
- Per-record calls are rate-limited at roughly 300 requests, then a pause of
  about five minutes. Undocumented. The existing client waits out
  `Retry-After`.
- The API can create and delete groups. **API deletes are not restorable**;
  the cleanup staged records into a group and a human bulk-deleted in the UI,
  which is.

VG:

- All villages share one schema; `person.villageId` tells them apart. The
  organization is a federation of thirteen villages, and anything org-wide
  (donors, Mailchimp) takes no village filter.
- Use the `active_volunteer` view; `volunteer.active` no longer exists.
- People created without the app's address resolver get no town or
  legislative districts. The import relied on running `backfill-towns` and
  `backfill-districts` afterward; a service cannot.
- A restore can reuse person ids, so any journal keyed on a VG person id must
  also check the person is still who it was.
- VG's disability vocabulary is fixed at five values; saving a person drops
  legacy entries outside it. That is intended, not data loss.

Mailchimp:

- VG manages the audience and its tags; a customer contact designs and sends
  the mailings.
- The plan has a billable contact ceiling (4,530 of 5,000 on 2026-11-02), so
  adds are not free.
- Never re-add someone who unsubscribed, bounced ("cleaned") or was archived.
- The audience requires first and last name, so organizations are written
  with `skip_merge_validation=true`.
- A 200 response is not proof; the existing tool reads back after each write.
- The existing Donor tag is applied to subscribed LGL donors and never
  removed by tooling.
- Spouses sharing one email become one contact.

Scale on 2026-11-08, for sizing only: about 960 person donors in LGL, of
which about 770 gave within five years; about 245 organizations; the import
linked about 400 to existing VG people and created about 365.
